Peer-reviewed articleCommunications Biology · 2026

lncRNAs contribute to caste differentiation as a regulatory layer in ants

Ding, G., Lin, F., Zheng, J. et al. Communications Biology (2026)

What this work asks

Queens and workers can share essentially the same genome yet develop different bodies and perform very different work. Across developmental transcriptomes from the pharaoh ant and a leaf-cutting ant, this study identifies lncRNAs whose caste bias becomes progressively canalized, then connects those patterns with tissue-specific protein-coding networks, RNA-interference experiments, and juvenile-hormone responses.

My contribution

I contributed to several parts of the bioinformatics analysis.

CHARMM-GUI System Builder

An AI Agent skill for checking CHARMM-GUI membrane-system inputs, recovering interrupted jobs, identifying downloaded packages, and verifying GROMACS files.

Problem
A completed browser page, a completed download, and a checked GROMACS topology are three different states; none can prove the other two.
Liao's contribution
Turned input checks, browser-job capture, long-job recovery, archive identification, and GROMACS file inspection into steps an agent can execute one by one.
Public evidence
GitHub v2.2.0 release, AGPL-3.0 source, and public documentation; the public repository contains 130 tests and publishes structural-environment, pose-preservation, restraint-handoff, and Receipt 2.0 closure gates.
Boundary
An unofficial community project that does not replace CHARMM-GUI, run production simulation, or approve a system for production MD automatically.
Current direction
Continue strengthening structural-environment, ligand-pose, and restraint-handoff checks while keeping system building, MD authorization, and scientific judgment separate.

An unofficial community project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by CHARMM-GUI, the Im Lab, or Lehigh University.

02Research Perspective

Proteins, small molecules and computation

“Living matter evades the decay to equilibrium.”

— Erwin Schrödinger, What Is Life?

My research spans ecology, insect behavior, evolution and multi-omics, extending into AI4Science. I now focus on AI-assisted drug discovery (AIDD), using structure comparisons, virtual screening and molecular dynamics to study protein–small molecule interactions and select candidates for experiments.

A protein can adopt different conformations, and the same molecule can have several proposed binding poses. I am interested in how those choices affect a comparison: which contacts change, whether a pose remains plausible during a simulation, and whether different methods support the same interpretation.

Molecular generation, virtual screening, docking and molecular dynamics contribute different kinds of information. To interpret their results, the input structures, parameters and selection decisions need to be available for review. A candidate also needs a practical route to synthesis or purchase and an appropriate assay. Computational scores and trajectories alone do not establish binding, activity or mechanism.

01

Protein conformations and molecular contacts

Research question

If the protein changes conformation, does a proposed small-molecule binding pose still make sense?

What the comparison involves

Comparing pocket geometry and molecular contacts can reveal why a prediction depends on a particular structure. Concentration, membranes, metabolism and competing molecules introduce further conditions when moving from a structural model to a cellular experiment.

02

Choosing candidates for experiments

Research question

When only a small number of compounds can be tested, what should determine their priority?

What the comparison involves

Docking scores are one part of that decision. Chemical plausibility, structural diversity, agreement between methods and the availability of compounds also affect whether a shortlist is useful to an experimental team.

03

Reproducing a computational comparison

Research question

Can another person trace a selected candidate back to its structure, settings and selection criteria?

What the comparison involves

Structure preparation, software versions, failed runs and manual selections can all change a comparison. Recording them makes it possible to repeat the calculation and examine why two runs disagree.

03Research Arc

From ecology, insects and omics to AIDD

“In nature, nothing exists alone.”

— Rachel Carson, Silent Spring
  1. 01Current direction

    AIDD & molecular design

    My current research focuses on proteins, small molecules and computational chemistry. It brings together molecular generation, virtual screening, docking and molecular dynamics to compare possible interactions and candidate compounds.

    The experimental follow-up remains part of the question: which compounds can be obtained, what should be measured, and which alternative explanations need testing?

  2. 02Computational transition

    AI for Biology & computational methods

    The transition from omics to AIDD shifted the focus of my computational work toward protein structures and small molecules. Structure preparation, candidate comparison and simulation analysis became central tasks.

    This work connects my background in biology and genomics with structural biology and chemistry. The questions now concern molecular geometry and interactions as well as variation in biological data.

  3. 03Earlier foundation

    Evolutionary genomics & multi-omics

    My earlier work includes evolutionary genomics, transcriptomics, epigenetics and heterosis. These areas examine how genetic variation and gene regulation relate to differences in phenotype.

    Interpreting an omics comparison requires attention to sample composition, batch effects and annotation. Those conditions matter when deciding whether a difference supports a biological explanation.

  4. 04Organisms and behavior

    Insects, behavior & adaptation

    Insects and behavior are also part of my research background. At this scale, the questions concern whole organisms and the relationship between behavior, development and environmental conditions.

    A behavioral measurement needs its context: what was measured, under which conditions, and what other behaviors could produce a similar value.

  5. 05Ecological background

    Ecology, biodiversity & living systems

    Ecology is part of my earlier research background, alongside an interest in biodiversity. It considers organisms in relation to other species, resources and environmental change.

    My present focus on molecules sits within this broader biological background. Molecular, organismal and ecological studies address different parts of a biological question and require different kinds of evidence.
