01

Prepare structures, set parameters, submit calculations

MolloM brings integrated docking and molecular dynamics tools into one interface: prepare structures, set parameters, submit calculations, then inspect results and logs.

  1. 01

    Configure Vina docking

    Engines have their own controls: search effort, seeds, CPU and GPU options, plus GROMACS runtime and MDP settings. Dedicated controls and native-protocol entries preserve detailed configuration for review before submission.

  2. 02

    Browse the run list

    Read projects, progress, result summaries and logs from a connected compute host. Keep notes, favourites, candidate comparisons and timestamped offline copies. For supported desktop projects, edit pending plans and review changes; submit only with separate permission and resource confirmation.

  3. 03

    Inspect candidate results

    Return to the structures and settings before deciding what to investigate next.

Actual development interfaces with demo settings and simulated tasks. No real computation was submitted.

02

Prepare at the desk. Follow up on your phone.

Desktop

Prepare proteins and ligands, inspect structures in 3D, configure boxes, engines and resources, then submit local or server jobs. Manage screening rounds, recover failed items, export results and work with GROMACS simulations and trajectory analysis. Docking and MD can be used independently.

03

Existing engines. A traceable record.

MolloM is not a new docking or MD engine. It calls existing programs and retains original output, parameters and attempts. Project packages carry selected inputs and results. Compatible AI agents can read status or prepare plans through MCP.

04

Where calculations run and data travels

Docking and simulations run on your computer or chosen server. Account connections can relay selected task status, result summaries and logs to your phone. Software installation and external services you enable also use the network.

Delegate the operations, not the scientific judgement

Docking scores and trajectories help compare candidates; they do not establish binding or mechanism on their own. Interpretation still depends on molecular identity, protonation, force fields, sampling and experimental evidence.
