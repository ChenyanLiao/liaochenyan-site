Precision as a daily ritual

GrindDialing

Dial in with less guesswork.

With a new bag of coffee, remember how the previous shot flowed so the next adjustment does not begin from a guess.

It does not decide whether the coffee tastes good. It simply keeps the last extraction from being forgotten.

01 / The first-shot problem

The recipe is familiar. The setting still has to be found.

On the same machine, a different coffee, roast, or rest time can change the flow completely. Experienced brewers still recalibrate; experience tells them what to watch, not how to land every first shot.

A common starting point is about 18 g of coffee, 36 g in the cup, and 25–30 seconds—roughly a 1:2 ratio. It is a useful ruler, not a law of coffee. Basket, temperature, pressure, distribution, and tamping can make the same numbers taste different.

A shot that runs too fast or barely drips need not lose all its value. Setting, time, and yield have already said something. GrindDialing carries that clue into the next cup instead of knocking it out with the puck.

04 / Learning your grinder

It learns this grinder, not a universal setting.

GrindDialing processes these records with a personalized Bayesian model. It maintains more than a setting: it keeps a relationship with uncertainty—how the current grind may affect flow, and how well the available data already describe that relationship. Every valid extraction revises the estimate.

That goes deeper than a fixed threshold or lookup table and suits grinders whose scales do not match. The public explanation stops here. Initial assumptions, probability updates, parameters, thresholds, and the search for the next recommendation remain part of the product core.

Impossible records do not become learning evidence directly; rare but plausible results are handled cautiously. Guidance can change as records accumulate, but taste keeps the final vote.

In the latest local Swift regression on 9 August 2026, 316 tests passed, 10 were skipped by design, and none failed. They cover input bounds, brew-method separation, persistence, and interface semantics. That shows the software behaves consistently; it does not establish a real-world recommendation accuracy rate, which is still being evaluated.

Coffee variety, roast, rest time, dose, distribution, tamping, temperature, pressure, and equipment condition all affect extraction. An unreliable zero point, missing records, or a recently changed machine weakens any recommendation. GrindDialing is not a perfect-coffee calculator.

Privacy

Privacy and data boundary

Core extraction, coffee, and grinder records are stored on the device; the current app privacy manifest declares no collected data and no tracking. Optional environment features use location and weather services only after permission and remain governed by system controls.
