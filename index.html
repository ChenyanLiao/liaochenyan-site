02Open-source scientific software—
Open-source scientific softwarev2.2.0 · AGPL-3.0

CHARMM-GUI System Builder

An auditable AI Agent skill for CHARMM-GUI membrane-system preparation, recovery, and GROMACS package validation.

Problem
Browser steps, asynchronous jobs, downloaded packages, and scientific approval fail at different boundaries; one 'complete' state is not enough evidence.
Liao's contribution
Organized input audit, task recovery, evidence capture, and GROMACS package validation into a traceable agent workflow with explicit stop conditions.
Public evidence
GitHub v2.2.0 release, AGPL-3.0 source, and public documentation; the public repository contains 130 tests and publishes structural-environment, pose-preservation, restraint-handoff, and Receipt 2.0 closure gates.
Boundary
An unofficial community project that neither replaces CHARMM-GUI nor grants production-MD approval automatically.
Current direction
Keep system building, MD authorization, and scientific judgment as distinct stages while changes remain versioned and independently verifiable.

An unofficial community project. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by CHARMM-GUI, the Im Lab, or Lehigh University.

02Research Perspective

Questions before answers

“Living matter evades the decay to equilibrium.”

— Erwin Schrödinger, What Is Life?

Science advances not only by producing answers, but by improving the questions we are able to ask.

Living systems are complex because causes and consequences cross scales. Computation can help organize that complexity, reveal patterns, and suggest hypotheses, but it cannot by itself decide what is biologically true.

I am interested in research that uses AI and quantitative models as instruments of inquiry: tools that make uncertainty visible, help prioritize evidence, and direct attention toward experiments that can genuinely change what we know.

01

Living systems across scales

Question

How do observations at one biological scale change the questions we ask at another?

Why it matters

Connecting scales matters because an elegant molecular explanation can still be incomplete without cellular, organismal, or environmental context.

02

Computation as an instrument of inquiry

Question

How can AI reduce a large space of possibilities without turning prediction into an unsupported conclusion?

Why it matters

The value of a model lies in the quality of the next question it enables, the evidence it helps prioritize, and the limits it makes explicit.

03

Tools that preserve scientific judgment

Question

What should scientific software reveal so that people can inspect, challenge, and improve a result?

Why it matters

Good tools should strengthen careful reasoning rather than hide assumptions behind speed, automation, or apparent certainty.

03Research Arc

A research path across scales

“In nature, nothing exists alone.”

— Rachel Carson, Silent Spring
  1. 01Current direction

    AI for Science, AIDD & molecular design

    Using computation to narrow large spaces of molecular possibilities into clearer hypotheses, priorities, and experiments worth considering.

    The aim is useful scientific triage, not a machine-generated substitute for biological evidence.

  2. 02Computational turn

    AI for Biology & scientific software

    Building models and tools that make complex biological information easier to inspect, compare, and turn into testable questions.

    Software is part of scientific reasoning because interfaces, records, and defaults shape what people can notice and challenge.

  3. 03Earlier foundation

    Evolutionary genomics & multi-omics

    Learning to connect inherited variation, regulation, phenotype, environment, and evolutionary history across high-dimensional data.

    This work sharpened my interest in representations that remain interpretable when the underlying biology is complicated.

  4. 04Organisms and behavior

    Insects, behavior & adaptation

    Observing how individuals sense, decide, interact, and respond to changing environments.

    Behavior made clear that biological variation is never only a number; it is expressed in context, time, and relation.

  5. 05Widest frame

    Ecology, biodiversity & living systems

    Beginning from relationships among organisms, communities, landscapes, and the environments that sustain them.

    It remains the broadest reminder in my work: even a precise molecular question belongs to a larger living system.

04About

I Am What I Am

“You can't connect the dots looking forward; you can only connect them looking backwards.”

— Steve Jobs

I work on AI for Science, AI for Biology, AIDD, and molecular design, and I also turn a few everyday needs into software I genuinely want to use.

01

My present interests center on AI for Science, AI for Biology, AIDD, molecular design, and scientific software. I am interested not only in what a model predicts, but in how a question is framed, what evidence is missing, and which next experiment could genuinely change our confidence.

02

AIDD matters because chemical possibility is far larger than experimental capacity. Computation can help organize that imbalance: prioritizing plausible directions, revealing uncertainty, and making expensive experimental attention more deliberate. It is most valuable when prediction and validation remain clearly separated.

03

My earlier path through ecology, insects, behavior, evolutionary genomics, transcriptomics, epigenetics, and heterosis still shapes how I think. It taught me that biological explanations become stronger when molecular detail, organismal context, environment, and history are allowed to speak to one another.

04

Independent apps and photography are not side notes to that work. They extend the same habit in different materials: observe carefully, preserve context, make uncertainty visible, and build something that helps a person decide what to do next.

Why a lobster?

I simply like this slightly absurd, forward-moving image. Research and software can be rigorous without requiring the person doing them to look solemn all the time. For me, the lobster is a playful reminder that however capable a tool becomes, people still decide where it should go.
